# Speed-Related Crashes and Occupant Injury Severity: A 2023 FARS Analysis

## Project Overview

This project examines the association between speed-related factors, roadway lighting conditions, and occupant injury severity in fatal motor-vehicle crashes in the United States in 2023. Using data from the Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS), the analysis applies Python-based data processing, exploratory data analysis, logistic regression, and interaction modeling to investigate how the association between speed-related crashes and severe or fatal injuries varies across lighting conditions.

## Research Questions

1. How are speed-related crash factors associated with severe or fatal occupant injuries in U.S. fatal crashes?
2. Does the association between speed-related status and injury severity vary across roadway lighting conditions?
3. Are the findings consistent across alternative model specifications and sensitivity analyses?

## Data Source

- **Dataset:** 2023 Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS)
- **Agency:** National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), United States
- **Unit of analysis:** Vehicle occupants
- **Initial eligible sample:** 81,269 occupant records
- **Primary complete-case analysis:** 75,806 occupant records
- **Sensitivity analysis:** 27,873 occupants with available travel-speed information

Raw FARS data files are not included in this repository. Users should obtain the data from the official NHTSA source and follow its documentation and data-use terms.

## Methodology

The analysis includes:

- Data inspection, cleaning, and missing-data assessment
- Definition of severe or fatal injury outcomes
- Classification of speed-related and non-speed-related cases
- Exploratory analysis of occupant injury severity
- Logistic regression with relevant occupant and crash characteristics
- Speed-by-lighting interaction analysis
- Robustness checks using alternative model specifications
- Statistical interpretation and visualization of results

### Injury Severity Outcome

Severe or fatal injury is defined using the FARS injury-severity codes 3 and 4. Codes 0–2 are grouped as non-severe for the primary binary outcome.

### Speed-Related Classification

Cases with `SPEEDREL = 0` are classified as not speed-related. Codes 2–5 are classified as speed-related. Unknown or unclassifiable codes are excluded from the primary comparison.

### Lighting Conditions

The analysis examines six lighting categories:

- Dark – lighted
- Dark – not lighted
- Dark – lighting unknown
- Dawn
- Daylight
- Dusk

Dark – lighted is the reference category for the lighting interaction analysis.

## Key Findings

- Severe or fatal injuries accounted for **74.51%** of occupants classified as speed-related, compared with **44.63%** of occupants classified as not speed-related in the analyzed sample.
- In the primary interaction model, the adjusted odds ratio for speed-related status varied across lighting categories, relative to the corresponding non-speed-related group.
- The joint test of the speed-by-lighting interaction was statistically significant (**Wald χ² = 29.85, df = 5, p = 1.58 × 10⁻⁵**).
- Sensitivity analyses were conducted to assess the consistency of the findings under alternative model specifications.

These results describe associations within the analyzed FARS sample and should not be interpreted as causal effects.

## Important Limitations

FARS documents fatal motor-vehicle crashes. This study therefore examines injury severity among occupants involved in fatal crashes; it does not estimate injury risk across all road crashes.

The observational design does not establish causation. Findings may also be affected by missing data, variable definitions, unmeasured factors, and the characteristics of the selected sample.
